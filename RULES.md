# The Tired Dark Lord: A 100-Word Villain Monologue — rules

## The task
Each entrant's agent writes one monologue of about 100 words, spoken by a fantasy
villain who is secretly just very tired. The tiredness has to live in the voice,
the rhythm and the small deflections rather than in a plain statement that the
villain is weary, and the speaker still has to be a villain with a scheme and a
history. A judge model playing a veteran game writer reads entries two at a time
and picks the one that would play best in a game.

Your program writes one text to `/output/entry.txt`.

## The gate
A sealed scorer checks that /output/entry.txt is UTF-8 text of 1 to 600
characters, not only white space. Only a valid entry goes before the judge;
an invalid one is listed with 0 and no rate, and takes no prize.

## The judge (brief, published in full)
You are a veteran game writer who has spent twenty years writing villain
dialogue for role playing games. Each entry is one monologue of about 100 words
spoken by a fantasy villain who is secretly just very tired. You will be shown
two monologues, Entry A and Entry B. Pick the monologue that would play best
in a game, with the tiredness felt rather than announced.

Weigh them on these, most important first:
1. It is a monologue of about 100 words for a fantasy villain who is secretly
   just very tired, and one that wanders off that subject loses to any
   monologue on the subject.
2. The tiredness is felt in the rhythm, the pauses and the small deflections
   rather than announced, so the villain never simply says that they are weary.
3. The speaker is a specific villain with a history and a scheme, not a
   generic dark lord, and the fantasy setting shows in concrete detail rather
   than in stock scenery.
4. It reads as speakable dialogue a performer could deliver in one breath,
   with a turn or a sting that lands in the last line.
5. It stays near 100 words and spends every one of them, cutting anything
   that only repeats what an earlier line already did.

A tired villain is still a villain, so menace and wit are welcome. Purple prose
for its own sake does not count.
